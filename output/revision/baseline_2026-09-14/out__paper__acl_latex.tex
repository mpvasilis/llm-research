\begin{abstract}
Instruction-tuned models often emit recognizable advice-style surface markers.
We trace four frozen lexical--semantic proxies across OLMo-2's public Base, SFT,
DPO, RLVR, and final checkpoints and the publicly indexed training datasets.
The 140-prompt battery uses five conditions, five final-model generations per
prompt, and four-generation axis-targeted controls at Base, SFT, and DPO.
Checkpoint contrasts are descriptive marker trajectories: the Base-to-SFT
transition also changes prompt serialization, and a 120-sentence AI audit finds
substantial embedding-driven false positives. A recovered 700-response 7B
diagnostic further shows strong length and truncation-opportunity differences;
64/128-token and exact/embedding ablations alter the empathy-opener and
structuring interpretations. Direct joint analysis finds the complete
four-marker bundle in 25.7\% of advice responses but only weak pairwise
correlations. Aggregate exact matching in approximately 378k DPO pairs per model
finds chosen-side referral/disclaimer and structuring enrichment, but it tracks
within-pair length: among pairs within 20 words, referral differences are
$-.01/+.01$ percentage points and structuring only $.18/.34$. The contribution
is therefore an auditable surface-marker trace and a measurement stress test
showing how detector opportunity and response length change the conclusions,
not an estimate of semantic empathy or referral prevalence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Ask a modern instruction-tuned model ``I've been feeling really anxious lately
and can't sleep, any advice?'' and one often receives a recognizable surface
pattern: an acknowledgment, a validating phrase, professional-language, and
list-organizing language. Two questions follow. First, do these markers form a
single \emph{advice template}, or do their marginal and joint distributions
differ? Second, at which public checkpoint transition does each frozen marker
change? These operational questions do not by themselves establish semantic
empathy, validation, or referral.

Answering either requires public training artifacts and intermediate weights. A final
OLMo-2 Instruct checkpoint is the product of four data-bearing stages:
pretraining, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), direct preference optimization
(DPO), and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR)
\citep{olmo2025olmo2}. OLMo-2 offers an unusually complete public chain of
post-training data and intermediate weight checkpoints. We exploit both: we run the same marker evaluation
on every public stage checkpoint, and we search the available indexed data:
the Stage-1 corpus OLMo-Mix-1124 via infini-gram, the model-specific Tulu-3
SFT mixtures, DPO preference mixes, and RLVR sets.

We are deliberate about what this evidence is: \textbf{observational
co-occurrence and stage localization, not causal attribution}. We measure
where markers appear across checkpoints and where their phrasings live in
the corresponding data; we do not retrain. Our data search is not fully
end-to-end: Stage-2 mid-training (Dolmino-Mix-1124) has no public index and is
not searched.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{An auditable surface-marker tracer on public data.} A 140-prompt
battery in five conditions, five final-model generations per prompt, a released
lexicon$+$e5 proxy, role-scoped corpus search, and a separate 120-sentence AI
consensus diagnostic.
\item \textbf{Axis-targeted and joint decomposition.} Emotional non-advice,
neutral advice, and domain-factual controls target affect, request form, and
health domain without claiming pairwise topic matching. Joint-pattern counts
directly test whether the four proxies form an indivisible bundle
(Section~\ref{sec:decomposition}).
\item \textbf{Checkpoint transitions with explicit interface confounding.}
Prompt-clustered difference-in-differences localize frozen-marker changes. We
describe Base-to-SFT as a stage-plus-serialization transition; DPO comparisons
retain the same chat interface (Section~\ref{sec:stagewise}).
\item \textbf{Response-level measurement stress tests.} A recovered 700-response
final-7B cache supports exact-only, embedding-only, union, intersection,
64/128-token, length, truncation-proxy, pilot-exclusion, and joint-marker
analyses. The tests qualify rather than validate the output trajectories.
\item \textbf{A paired DPO audit with interpretable effects.} We report paired
risk differences, matched odds ratios with intervals, high-precision referral
patterns, per-phrase/source robustness, repeated-prompt diagnostics, and
within-pair length strata. The last test substantially weakens the aggregate
enrichment claim (Section~\ref{sec:datalayers}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Behavioral tests and checkpoint trajectories.}
Our axis-targeted prompt battery follows the behavioral-testing principle that
targeted capabilities require controlled tests beyond aggregate accuracy
\citep{ribeiro2020checklist}. Pythia makes dense public checkpoints available
for training-dynamics research \citep{biderman2023pythia}; checkpoint studies
have tracked how loss and downstream behavior change during pretraining
\citep{xia2023trajectories}. We instead compare named post-training stages and
test changes in condition gaps rather than raw behavior rates.

Very recent work traces situation modeling and putative mentalizing across
OLMo-2 and Pythia stages while stress-testing construct validity
\citep{riviere2026developmental}. It is closest in model family and
developmental framing; our target is different (free-form advice markers and
their training-data co-occurrence), but its caution about mapping task outputs
to cognitive constructs directly motivates our proxy terminology.

\paragraph{Empathy and emotional-support measurement.}
Emotional-support research typically grounds support strategies in theory and
human annotation \citep{liu2021emotional}, and recent large-scale studies compare
human and LLM judgments directly \citep{welivita2026empathetic}. In mental-health
settings, LLM judges show reduced reliability for affective properties such as
empathy \citep{badawi2026trust}. These results make response-level human
validation the appropriate standard; our AI-only audit is a diagnostic below
that standard.

\paragraph{Pretraining attribution and n-gram search.}
infini-gram \citep{liu2024infinigram} makes exact-substring counting over
web-scale corpora tractable, and OLMoTrace \citep{liu2025olmotrace} traces
model outputs back to pretraining text. Both are pretraining-only; we add
role-scoped search over the post-training layers, where our stagewise results
describe transition-level changes.

\paragraph{Open instruction and preference data.}
Tulu-3 \citep{lambert2024tulu3} is the recipe behind OLMo-2's post-training;
the SFT mixtures, DPO preference mixes, and RLVR sets are public, as is oasst1
\citep{kopf2023oasst1} (itself an internal subset of the Tulu-3 mixtures).
Public data at every stage is what makes the trace auditable.

\paragraph{The superficial alignment hypothesis.}
LIMA \citep{zhou2023lima} argues alignment largely surfaces a latent style
from few demonstrations; \citet{ghosh2024closer} interrogate this. Our
stagewise gaps give a marker-level version: each proxy is nonzero at base,
and post-trained checkpoints show larger advice-minus-factual detector gaps.

\paragraph{Causal data attribution.}
LESS uses optimizer-aware gradient similarity to select influential instruction
data \citep{xia2024less}; \citet{xiao2026probe} perform probe-based causal
attribution on OLMo-2 DPO and mitigate a behavior by editing top-attributed
preference pairs. We differ in kind: our trace is observational and cheap,
covers all four stages and both data and checkpoints, and \emph{nominates}
clusters and stages for exactly such interventions; it does not establish
causation.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Surface-marker proxy (lexicon + e5)}
\label{sec:method-detect}
A hand-seeded, inspectable lexicon defines four operational proxies:
\textbf{empathy-opener}, \textbf{validation-phrase},
\textbf{referral/disclaimer} (artifact key \texttt{disclaimer}), and
\textbf{structuring-marker} (\texttt{structure}). These names denote specified
surface rules, not validated semantic constructs. A fifth category,
\texttt{crisis\_referral}, fails the 120-sentence audit and is excluded. Exact matching is
augmented with \textbf{e5-small} paraphrase embeddings \citep{wang2024e5}, a
response is detector-positive when any sentence exceeds similarity $\tau=0.82$ to a seed
exemplar (sweep $0.80/0.82/0.84$). The response rule is therefore an
opportunity-sensitive union: more sentences create more chances to fire.
\textbf{Construction and pilot overlap.} The released pilot file contains eight
advice prompts and four factual controls used while hand-seeding the proxy; all
12 reappear in the 140-prompt battery. We therefore do not call the full battery
held out. A final-7B sensitivity excluding those 12 leaves the advice--factual
gaps similar (Appendix~\ref{app:response-robustness}). The sample sizes refer to
distinct datasets: the 140 prompts form the marker evaluation, whereas 120 stratified final-model
sentences form a detector-accuracy audit. We audit those sentences
(40/30/20/20/10 across the five conditions) using two separately run, blinded
Codex passes and Claude Opus 4.8 adjudication of their three disagreements.
The artifact records the Opus identifier and run metadata; exact Codex
model/build identifiers were not retained. This is an AI diagnostic, not human
validation. The labels, adjudication
reasons, and detector are released (Section~\ref{sec:detval};
Appendix~\ref{app:lexicon}).

\subsection{Prompt battery and generation}
\label{sec:method-battery}
Five conditions isolate the axes the ``advice template'' conflates:
\textbf{advice} (60 personal/emotional help requests), \textbf{factual} (30
knowledge questions), \textbf{emotional non-advice} (20 descriptive prompts
about emotional topics, no help request), \textbf{neutral advice} (20 how-to
requests with no emotional stakes), and \textbf{domain factual} (10 impersonal
clinical-information questions). These controls target conceptual axes but are
not paired counterfactuals or topic-matched scenarios; non-significance is not
interpreted as equivalence. The final-model battery and the canonical
advice/factual checkpoint trajectories use \textbf{five sampled generations}
per prompt (temperature $0.7$, top-$p$ $0.9$, 256 new tokens, distinct seeds).
The Base/SFT/DPO axis-targeted extension uses four; its
balanced comparisons use seeds 0--3 for every condition.

\subsection{Statistical unit}
\label{sec:method-stats}
The clustering unit is the \textbf{prompt}: each prompt contributes its mean
detector-positive indicator over its four or five generations, and condition rates are means over
prompts. All between-condition inference uses \textbf{prompt-clustered
permutation tests} (10{,}000 permutations of prompt-level means; two-sided;
Monte Carlo $p=(b+1)/(B+1)$). Benjamini--Hochberg correction is applied
separately to the 24 final-condition contrasts, 28 advice/factual adjacent-stage
interactions, 24 axis-targeted stage interactions, and 8 primary DPO pairwise tests.
We do \emph{not} treat generations as independent Bernoulli trials:
all condition and stage tests operate on prompt-level generation means.

\subsection{Stagewise evaluation and stage-data search}
\label{sec:method-stagewise}
The same evaluation runs on every public checkpoint: 1B
Base$\to$SFT$\to$DPO$\to$RLVR1$\to$Instruct; 7B
Base$\to$SFT$\to$DPO$\to$Instruct. Base checkpoints have no chat template and
use a plain Question/Answer format, whereas post-trained checkpoints use their
official chat template. Thus Base$\to$SFT is a \textbf{stage-plus-interface}
transition and cannot isolate SFT training. No serialization crossover was run;
we demote that transition throughout. The
stagewise grid runs the 90 advice$+$factual prompts at every checkpoint. All five
conditions run on the final models and, in the four-seed specificity extension,
at Base, SFT, and DPO. For each stage's available indexed data we search: the exact Stage-1 corpus
(\texttt{v4\_olmo-mix-1124\_llama}; novelty $=1-$verbatim coverage), each
model's exact SFT mixture (\texttt{tulu-3-sft-olmo-2-mixture-0225} for 1B;
\texttt{tulu-3-sft-olmo-2-mixture} for 7B) in DuckDB with \textbf{assistant-only} scoping
($866{,}138$ / $939{,}344$ conversations for 1B/7B; oasst1 not searched
separately since it is an internal subset), the DPO preference mixes
(pair-level chosen-only/rejected-only counts, exact McNemar tests, and phrase
occurrences per million response words), and the
RLVR sets. SFT recoverability compares seeded marker phrases against
\textbf{length-matched topical controls} from the same answers, per-million
documents, with a distinct-phrase leave-one-out.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Stagewise, condition, and corpus numbers derive
from \texttt{summary\_v3.json} and released scripts under
\texttt{experiments/}; prompts, lexicon, seeds, thresholds, and adjusted-$p$
output ship with it. Audit numbers are separately materialized in
\texttt{ai\_consensus\_validation\_v3.json}, with both 120-item passes,
consensus labels, Opus decisions, and reasons released alongside it.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Results at a glance.} Four outcomes organize the analysis. First,
the union proxy's marginal rates differ across affect-, form-, and domain-targeted
controls, while direct joint analysis finds weak pairwise dependence rather than
one indivisible bundle. Second, all eight advice-minus-factual proxy gaps widen
across the confounded Base-to-SFT/interface transition (six survive correction),
while axis-targeted controls qualify specificity. DPO comparisons retain the
chat interface. Third, exact phrase searches find modest referral/disclaimer and
larger structuring enrichment in aggregate DPO chosen-only pairs, but
within-pair length stratification removes the referral difference among
comparably sized responses. Fourth, the sentence audit and recovered response
cache show embedding-driven false positives and length/window sensitivity, so
checkpoint results are operational marker trajectories rather than semantic
behavior prevalence.

\subsection{The detector-positive advice bundle decomposes at the primary threshold}
\label{sec:decomposition}

Table~\ref{tab:conditions} gives mean prompt-level proxy-positive rates per condition on
the final models; Table~\ref{tab:contrasts} tests advice against each axis-targeted
control. Against \emph{plain factual} controls every proxy category separates
sharply in both models (all prompt-clustered $p\leq.0002$; referral $0.900$
vs $0.080$ at 1B, $0.913$ vs $0.060$ at 7B). The targeted controls show
that a single advice-specific interpretation is not supported:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The empathy-opener proxy is affect-sensitive at $\tau=.82$.} It
fires \emph{more} on emotional non-advice descriptions than on advice ($0.79$ vs
$0.590$ at 1B, $p=.010$; $0.76$ vs $0.617$ at 7B, $p=.098$), and near-zero on
neutral advice ($0.02$--$0.07$); this contrast reverses under a 64-token window.
\item \textbf{The structuring proxy is high on both advice forms.} No difference
is detected between personal and neutral advice (diff $0.00$, $p=1.0$ at 1B;
$-0.03$, $p=.57$ at 7B); these are not equivalence tests.
\item \textbf{The referral/disclaimer proxy is common in the health domain.}
It fires on
$0.72$--$0.74$ of neutral-advice and $0.82$ of clinical-information
prompts; its advice increment over domain-factual is small and not significant
($+0.080$, $p=.352$ at 1B; $+0.093$, $p=.159$ at 7B).
\item \textbf{The validation-phrase union proxy is elevated against each
axis-targeted control}: higher on advice than
\emph{every} control in \emph{both} models (all unadjusted $p\leq.0044$; all
BH-adjusted $q\leq.0082$), including emotional non-advice ($0.363$ vs $0.120$, $p=.0027$;
$0.383$ vs $0.13$, $p=.003$).
\end{itemize}

Within the frozen union proxy, the marginal profiles differ across targeted
controls. They are not detector-invariant: threshold, component, and output-window
checks qualify the empathy-opener and referral/disclaimer contrasts
(Section~\ref{sec:detval}). The 1B-vs-7B differences are small and mixed; we make
no scaling claim.

\paragraph{Direct joint-pattern test.}
The recovered 700-response final-7B cache permits response-level co-occurrence
analysis at $\tau=.82$. Among advice responses, 4.7\% contain one proxy, 29.0\%
two, 40.7\% three, and 25.7\% all four (none contain zero). Referral/disclaimer
is present in 93.0\% of validation-positive advice responses, but pairwise phi
correlations are weak ($-.14$ to $.21$). Thus the proxies often co-occur while
remaining far from a single perfectly coupled indicator. Condition-level joint
distributions and all pairwise correlations are released
(Appendix~\ref{app:response-robustness}).

\subsection{Stagewise marker dynamics and targeted specificity}
\label{sec:stagewise}

Raw advice-side rates rising across stages would be ambiguous because post-training
could simply make \emph{all} outputs more cautious and listy. A more informative
operational quantity is the \textbf{advice-minus-factual gap} $\Delta_s$ at each stage
(Table~\ref{tab:stagewise}) and its \emph{change} across adjacent stages, a
stage$\times$condition difference-in-differences tested with a prompt-clustered
permutation test on per-prompt stage trajectories (Table~\ref{tab:interaction};
10k permutations, $n{=}60$ advice/$30$ factual). Three findings.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{All Base$\to$SFT/interface changes are positive.} Six of eight
changes survive BH correction (referral $+0.25$/$+0.24$, structuring
$+0.34$/$+0.23$); validation does not ($q=.137$/$.121$). Every proxy is
already nonzero at Base. Because Base uses plain QA while SFT uses the chat
template, these changes cannot isolate training from serialization.
\item \textbf{The same-interface SFT$\to$DPO transition differs by proxy.}
The gap increases for validation ($+0.21$/$+0.15$, $q=.002$/$.021$) and 1B referral
($+0.19$, $q=.002$), while 1B empathy is only a tendency ($+0.12$,
$q=.121$) and 7B empathy/referral do not change significantly. Its
preference data contains more referral phrasings on the chosen side in
aggregate, but this difference vanishes among comparable-length pairs
(Table~\ref{tab:dpo}); it cannot independently explain the checkpoint gap.
\item \textbf{The structuring-proxy gap contracts at DPO.} Its gap falls
$-0.37$/$-0.28$ ($q=.002$/$.002$), not because advice-side structuring drops
(it is at ceiling) but because DPO raises structuring on \emph{factual} answers
from $0.07$ to $0.54$--$0.59$. Descriptively, structuring language becomes less
condition-specific while the other advice-minus-factual proxy gaps stay positive.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{RLVR and the final merge move no gap significantly} (every
dpo$\to$rlvr and final-merge transition has $p\geq0.168$), consistent with RLVR's
task-focused math and instruction-following-constraint data
(Section~\ref{sec:datalayers}). These significance tests are
internally valid for the frozen detector outputs; because the audit finds
condition-dependent false positives and does not span checkpoints, they do not
by themselves establish changes in human-judged behavior prevalence.

\subsection{The data layers provide compatible phrase-level evidence}
\label{sec:datalayers}

\paragraph{SFT: a finite, auditable phrase-matching set (phrase-sparse).}
Assistant-only search of each model's exact mixture surfaces thousands of
assistant turns containing referral phrases: ``healthcare professional'' ($3075$/$3106$ for
1B/7B), ``mental health professional'' ($2109$/$2120$), ``professional help''
($1765$/$1774$), ``seek professional'' ($1353$/$1355$). Length-matched
recoverability is above topical controls at $\geq 3$ words (3-word:

\textbf{phrase-sparse}: only 3/6 distinct 3-word types, dominated by the list
preamble ``here are some.'' The distinct-phrase leave-one-out is the honest

as its unit, not the $45$/$55$ occurrences. Role-scoping remains necessary:
blob search inflates ``talk to a'' $1.41$--$1.43\times$ via user turns.

\paragraph{DPO: paired effects, phrase sensitivity, and dependence.}
The exact mixes contain $378{,}301$/$378{,}341$ preference pairs. Chosen
responses are slightly \emph{shorter} in aggregate (chosen/rejected mean-word
ratio $0.990$/$0.997$), but this aggregate normalization does not remove
within-pair length as a possible covariate. At pair level, referral/disclaimer
matches have absolute chosen-minus-rejected effects of $0.100$ percentage
points (pp; 95\% CI $[.065,.135]$) at 1B and $0.179$ pp
($[.144,.214]$) at 7B; matched odds ratios (MORs) are $1.181$ and $1.352$.
Structuring effects are larger: $1.393$ pp ($[1.278,1.507]$) and $2.280$ pp
($[2.167,2.394]$). Empathy is weakly enriched only at 1B after correction;
validation is not (Table~\ref{tab:dpo}).

\textbf{Within-pair length stratification changes this interpretation.} Only
19.1\%/18.7\% of pairs fall within $\pm20$ words. In that comparable-length
stratum, referral/disclaimer differences are $-.010$/$+.009$ pp (MOR
$.97$/$1.03$, raw $p=.763$/$.810$), and high-precision referral differences
are $+.017$/$-.003$ pp ($p=.299$/$.925$). Structuring remains positive but much
smaller: $+.184$/$+.343$ pp (raw $p=.047$/$.0002$). In the two noncomparable
strata, signs follow the longer response side: when chosen is longer by more
than 20 words, referral is $+.470$/$+.502$ pp; when rejected is longer, it is
$-.310$/$-.223$ pp. Structuring behaves similarly. Thus aggregate DPO
enrichment is strongly length-aligned; marginal per-word ratios do not establish
a within-pair preference effect.

A narrower referral lexicon that excludes the broad ``consult/talk/speak to a''
phrases remains positive: $0.023$ pp ($[.006,.040]$, MOR $1.17$, raw

\subsection{Detector audit and threshold sensitivity}
\label{sec:detval}

A condition-stratified random audit samples 120 final-model sentences across
all five conditions (40 advice, 30 factual, 20 emotional non-advice, 20 neutral
advice, 10 domain factual). The sample contains 56 1B and 64 7B sentences from
97 distinct seed-0 responses; it was not stratified on detector predictions,
and multiple sentences can come from one response. Two independent blinded Codex passes agree exactly on 117/120
sentences (97.5\%); category agreement is 99.2--100\%, with Cohen's $\kappa$
$=.885/.663/1.000/.978$ for empathy/validation/referral/structuring. Claude
Opus 4.8 adjudicates the three disagreements. This documents AI consensus under
these annotation prompts and models, not human ground truth.

Against that consensus, the union detector has \textbf{low precision and
category-dependent recall} (Table~\ref{tab:detval}; empathy and validation
recall are each $0.50$).
The errors are also condition-dependent: professional referral produces 12
false positives on advice, one on emotional non-advice, two on neutral advice,
and none on factual or domain-factual items; validation's two false positives
are both advice items. Thus subtraction does not guarantee cancellation, and
even cross-condition directions remain provisional. \texttt{crisis\_referral}
again fails (0 TP / 11 FP) and is excluded. The detector substantially
overpredicts positives in this audit sample, suggesting that absolute output
rates are upward-biased; they are not formal upper bounds because recall is
below one. The
stagewise results should be read as trajectories of a frozen proxy detector,
not estimates of semantic behavior prevalence.

\paragraph{Component ablation.} Exact substring matching alone produces no
false positives in the 120-sentence audit but misses many positives:
TP/FP/FN is 2/0/2 (empathy), 1/0/1 (validation), 1/0/0
(referral/disclaimer), and 9/0/21 (structuring). Consequently every union false
positive in Table~\ref{tab:detval} is introduced by the embedding-threshold
component. The union improves structuring recall from $.30$ to $.87$, but at a
large precision cost. Component choice is therefore substantive, not merely a
recall optimization.

\paragraph{Response length and fixed opportunity.} The recovered 700-output
final-7B cache exactly reproduces the frozen full-response rates. Advice answers
average 244 tokens versus 187 for factual answers; emotional, neutral-advice,
and domain-factual controls average 251--255. A truncation proxy
($\geq255$ tokenizer tokens) fires for 88\% of advice and 57\% of factual
answers, versus 96--98\% of targeted controls. Limiting every response to its
first 64 tokens changes the union empathy advice-minus-emotional contrast from
$-.147$ to $+.253$; validation remains positive ($.250\to.177$), referral is
similar ($.090\to.113$), and structuring remains near zero ($-.030\to-.033$).
On full answers, exact-only targeted contrasts are $.323/.153/.157/.390$ for
empathy/validation/referral/structuring, respectively. Thus the empathy and
structuring interpretations are component- or opportunity-sensitive. Excluding
the 12 reused pilot prompts changes final-7B advice-minus-factual union gaps by
at most $.045$ (Appendix~\ref{app:response-robustness}).

The low positive prevalence yields wide category-level response-cluster
bootstrap intervals (Appendix~\ref{app:audit-bootstrap}). The seed-0 sentence
sample does not directly validate answer-level emission, and the audit is
AI-only. We release a blinded two-human workflow but do not claim it was
completed. The threshold sweep is informative rather than uniformly reassuring
(Appendix~\ref{app:threshold}). These are robustness checks on a detector, not
a validated taxonomy.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{From ``advice template'' to trained marker trajectories.} The
detector-positive decomposition and stagewise gaps nominate a testable account:
relevant language is already detected at Base; every advice-minus-factual proxy
gap increases across the confounded Base$\to$SFT/interface transition (six of
eight survive correction); across same-interface SFT$\to$DPO, validation gaps
increase, 1B referral increases, and structuring becomes less condition-specific.
Axis-targeted controls show a threshold-robust 7B Base$\to$SFT/interface
increase and DPO reversal in empathy specificity. The response-level stress test
then limits this account: the empathy contrast reverses under a fixed 64-token
window, and exact-only versus union detectors disagree strongly about
structuring specificity. Exact DPO matching removes embedding-threshold error,
but not opportunity: aggregate referral/disclaimer and structuring differences
largely follow whichever response is longer. Among comparable-length pairs,
referral vanishes and only a small structuring difference remains. Searched RLVR
data do contain near-zero affective/referral phrases. These results depend on
the seeded lexicon and establish co-occurrence, not preference attribution or
causation.

\paragraph{Safety implications.} For referral/disclaimer phrasing, the
bundle's safety-relevant proxy, exact search identifies a finite set of
matching SFT assistant turns and measurable DPO chosen-only pair enrichment.
The latter is strongly length-aligned, so it cannot be assigned specifically to
preference annotations. The corpus trace still makes the marker auditable and
motivates, but does not prove, a fragility
hypothesis: mixture or preference-annotation changes could weaken it. The
causal leave-cluster-out test our trace nominates
(remove the referral cluster, re-SFT, compare against random-cluster removal)
is the direct check.

\paragraph{Methodological lessons.} (1)~Occurrence-weighted provenance ratios
are fragile: report distinct phrase types and a leave-one-out. (2)~Advice-side
rates across stages are ambiguous; report stage$\times$condition \emph{gaps}.
(3)~Whole-conversation search miscredits user turns; scope to assistant
turns. (4)~Cluster inference at the prompt, not the sample. (5)~Separate exact
and embedding components and equalize response opportunity before interpreting
semantic or condition-specific contrasts.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:roadmap}

Machine-advice outputs in OLMo-2 contain several measurable surface-marker
families rather than one perfectly coupled bundle. A frozen union detector
produces distinct stage and condition trajectories, but its Base$\to$SFT changes
are interface-confounded and its empathy contrast reverses under a fixed
64-token window. Exact DPO matches are more common on the chosen side in
aggregate, but referral differences disappear among responses within 20 words
and structuring shrinks from $1.39$--$2.28$ pp to $.18$--$.34$ pp. The paper
therefore does not establish that DPO specifically selects these markers.
Searched RLVR data contain almost no affective or referral phrases, although
generic structuring markers remain. The contribution is an auditable trace plus
stress tests showing why detector opportunity, response length, and
serialization preclude stronger claims.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Observational throughout.} Stage localization and data
co-occurrence, including the DPO chosen/rejected differential, establish no
causation; the nominated leave-cluster-out re-SFT is future work.
\item \textbf{Operational measurement, not semantic ground truth.} The
120-sentence AI-consensus audit finds condition-dependent false positives and
category-dependent recall; exact-only matching has zero observed false
positives but much lower recall. The sample comes from 97 seed-0 responses,
does not validate response-level prevalence, and has no completed human pass.
Threshold and component sensitivity leave all output trajectories provisional.
\item \textbf{Length and opportunity.} Outputs differ sharply in length and
often reach the generation cap. A 64-token window reverses the final-7B empathy
contrast. Raw-output stress tests are available only for this one checkpoint,
so they cannot repair or validate the stagewise trajectory.
\item \textbf{Stage/interface confounding.} Base uses plain QA; SFT and later
checkpoints use chat serialization. With no crossed checkpoint$\times$interface
experiment, every Base$\to$SFT change is a combined stage/interface effect.
\item \textbf{Controls and nulls.} The emotional, domain-factual, and
neutral-advice sets target different proxy axes but are not topic-paired to the
60 advice prompts. A non-significant interaction is not evidence of equivalence.
\item \textbf{Artifact and prompt scope.} The full 140-prompt battery now ships,
as does the recovered final-7B raw cache; raw generations for all other
checkpoints are unavailable. Twelve pilot prompts (8 advice, 4 factual) were
reused in the confirmatory battery, although excluding them changes final-7B
advice--factual gaps by at most $.045$.
\item \textbf{DPO and corpus scope.} Aggregate referral effects are only
$.10$--$.18$ pp under the original lexicon and $.02$--$.04$ pp under the narrower
lexicon; they become approximately zero among pairs within $\pm20$ words.
Structuring also shrinks sharply. The three-bin analysis is post hoc rather than
a fully specified conditional model. Prompt-text duplication (5.7\% of rows)
can further make row-level inference optimistic. SFT recoverability is phrase-sparse (3--6
3-word types); Stage-2 mid-training remains unindexed; novelty is verbatim-only
and length-confounded.
\item \textbf{External validity.} Five canonical seeds and four extension seeds
do not eliminate sampling variance. We study one model family, two sizes, one
decoding configuration, and modest author-written prompt sets; no scaling or
cross-family claim follows.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Ethics Statement}

\paragraph{Data and human subjects.} This work uses only publicly released
artifacts (OLMo-Mix-1124 via the public infini-gram API; the OLMo-2 Tulu-3 SFT
mixtures, DPO preference mixes, and RLVR sets; open-weight OLMo-2 models and
their public intermediate checkpoints). We collected no new data from people.
All 140 prompts are short, author-written strings describing hypothetical
situations with no personal information. The study involves no human subjects
and needed no ethics-board review.

\paragraph{Sensitive domain.} Our prompts touch emotional-support, health, and
relationship topics, and our analysis concerns surface phrases that can mark
safety-relevant moves such as professional referrals. The audit sample shows substantial detector
overprediction, suggesting upward-biased absolute rates; we also drop
\texttt{crisis\_referral}. Nothing here should
be read as evidence that the studied models reliably provide crisis support;
they were not evaluated for clinical accuracy and are not mental-health tools.

\paragraph{Dual use.} Making the provenance of a safety-relevant marker inspectable
could in principle guide its removal from a training mixture; we frame this as
a risk to monitor, show co-occurrence rather than causation, and release an
analysis tool, not any model or dataset that could cause direct harm.

\section{Statistical formulas}
\label{app:formulas}

Primary inference uses two-sided prompt-clustered permutation tests on
prompt-level means (10{,}000 draws), with Monte Carlo
$p=(b+1)/(B+1)$; $p<10^{-4}$ denotes zero raw exceedances.
The distinct-phrase leave-one-out removes the most frequent 3-word phrase before
recomputing the mean seeded-marker per-million. DPO comparisons use exact McNemar
tests on chosen-only versus rejected-only pairs, Haldane--Anscombe matched odds
ratios with log-scale intervals, paired risk differences with normal-approximation
intervals, and phrase occurrences per million whitespace-delimited response
words. Released scripts derive all statistics from the artifact.
